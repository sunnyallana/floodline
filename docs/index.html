Floodline · open research and software

Where should flood responders go first?

Floodline turns a satellite flood map, the road network, buildings, population and photos from field teams into a ranked map of 0.1 km² hexagons: which neighbourhoods are hit, and which can a truck no longer reach. Every claim on this page is checked against independent damage evidence from Copernicus EMS.

One map, two questions

A district flood coordinator needs to know where the damage and the people are, and how to get there. Floodline scores every 0.1 km² hexagon on exposure (buildings and people in the water), hazard (how much of it is flooded) and access (how much the flood lengthens the drive from the nearest fire station or hospital, or cuts it off), and fuses them into one priority. The closed roads and cut-off areas are also drawn as their own layer, because they decide whether to send a truck or a boat.

Six floods, four continents

Copernicus EMS analysts graded damage building by building after these floods, from post-event imagery. That grading never sees Floodline's inputs or outputs, so it is an independent test. Every town with at least 20 damaged or destroyed buildings is ranked under one protocol.

What finds the damage

Share of each town's damaged or destroyed buildings that fall in its top 10 % of hexagons, averaged over towns, with 95 % intervals from a spatial block bootstrap. Random ranking scores 0.10. Pooled, the leading scores sit inside each other's intervals; what separates them is how they do on their worst flood, below.

Every single layer fails somewhere

The same measure within each flood. A layer that looks good pooled can collapse on one kind of flood: water extent in the riverine floods, where the whole plain is wet; building counts in Moramanga.

Fitted on five floods, tested on the sixth

The scoring rule (its form, weights and exposure measure) is chosen on all events but one and scored on the held-out event. This is the out-of-sample test.

Roads under water

A road counts as closed when at least t metres of it lie in the flood. Checked against the analysts' road-damage grades on every graded town.

Limits

  • Building damage is a proxy for where help is needed; it does not measure access or people stranded on roofs.
  • The benchmark covers floods EMS graded between 2021 and 2023; graded towns are where analysts looked, not a random sample.
  • OpenStreetMap and building footprints are today's maps, not the map on the day of the flood.
  • The 300-image depth benchmark was labelled with an AI assistant; a blind human review is in progress.
  • No coordinator has used the system in a live flood yet.
